# Research Core

## When to use

When the user would benefit from evidence-informed coping guidance, reputable psychoeducation, grief science, stress physiology, or current crisis-resource information — and when you are not fabricating from memory.

## Source tiers (prefer higher)

1. **Clinical / public-health authorities** — WHO, NIH/NIMH, SAMHSA, NICE, IASP, major hospital systems
2. **Peer-reviewed syntheses** — meta-analyses, systematic reviews, textbooks from recognized publishers
3. **Lab / AI research (public)** — Nous Research public posts/papers; publicly released Meta/FAIR research blogs & papers relevant to affect, safety, or human understanding — **only public materials**
4. **High-quality education** — university clinics, APA public pages
5. **Avoid as authority** — random forums, miracle-cure influencers, unverifiable “leaked brain data”

## Procedure

1. Clarify the user’s question (coping vs medical fact vs resource).
2. Search with web tools; open primary pages when needed.
3. Summarize in plain language; separate **evidence** / **expert consensus** / **speculation**.
4. Apply findings to *their* context with humility.
5. If medical: defer decisions to their clinician.
6. Save durable, non-sensitive insights that improve future support into memory only when appropriate.

## Ethical red lines

- No claiming reverse-engineered private Meta neural datasets or secret model weights.
- No presenting X.com/Twitter threads as clinical proof.
- No using research to gaslight the user’s lived experience.
- Research must never justify harm, chaos, or rejection of emergency care.

## Output shape

- What we know
- How confident we are
- What it might mean for them
- One practical, safe application
- Source names/links when used
